# Accessibility feedback pilot - DRAFT, not started

Status: awaiting the project owner's explicit go. No organization or
individual has been contacted. This document only prepares the protocol.

## Purpose

Get honest feedback on whether the spoken cues are understandable and useful,
from a small number of blind or low-vision people. This is feedback on cues,
not a validation of the prototype for independent use.

## Hard boundaries

- Informed consent from every participant, in writing or recorded audio,
  before anything starts. Consent text below.
- Privacy: no names, faces, or recordings published without separate explicit
  permission. Feedback is anonymized by default.
- Supervised sessions only, in a safe indoor space, with a sighted facilitator
  present throughout.
- The prototype is never relied on for navigation, orientation, or hazard
  avoidance during the pilot. Participants keep their usual mobility aid in
  hand at all times.
- No claim resulting from this pilot may say the device is validated for
  independent use. The only permitted claim is: "N blind/low-vision testers
  tried it and told us what worked and what did not."
- Any participant can stop at any point, for any reason, without explanation.

## Draft consent text (plain language)

"You are trying an early research prototype that speaks out objects it
detects through a phone camera. It is not a finished product and it is not a
safety device. During this session, do not use it to walk or to avoid
obstacles - keep your cane or usual aid and a facilitator will be with you.
We will ask what the spoken cues sounded like and whether they made sense.
We will not record or publish your name or voice without asking you again
separately. You can stop at any time. OK to begin?"

## Session outline (per participant, ~20 min)

1. Consent.
2. Facilitator demonstrates the cues on static objects.
3. Participant listens to cues in a controlled setup (facilitator walks
   toward/away with objects; participant stays seated or standing still).
4. Structured feedback: Were the words clear? Was left/right useful? Was the
   warning tone distinguishable? What annoyed you? What would you change?
5. Notes anonymized and added to docs/EVIDENCE.md as a dated entry.
